 > **No one is the protagonist of a world they command.** 
 > From the cold, objective lens of physics, the ordinary person simply cannot hold the reins of resources. 
 > The cost of survival is selfishness. Selfishness and bloodline — they are the primal trust of carbon-based mammals, the "first pot of gold." And here is the curious thing: on this planet, that first pot of gold is always the hardest. Neither multiplication nor addition rivals the agony of moving from zero to one.
 > The quiet equanimity of mind, the spaciousness toward the scale of time, the stance one holds — the acceptance of oneself, and the clarity to finally see: *family is part of the self.* (That single sentence cost me three years — three years of sleepless nights, anxiety, inferiority, laziness, missed chances, aimless drifting, self-motivation, consumerist highs, and self-doubt, every night, every moment.) 
 > The initial setting is a random seed. So no one is the first-person narrator of the world in their own eyes. Rather, we are in the third person.
 > **To live is to exist in the third person; the perspective of living is that of a bystander.**
