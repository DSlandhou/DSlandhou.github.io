The Boy Who Built Worlds
I loved LEGO as a child. I loved those little plastic soldier toys, staging battles between them. My childhood was happy — truly happy. I spent my days in the courtyard spinning Beyblade tops and playing with Ultraman figures. I watched Armor Hero, Detective Conan, Digimon, B-Robo Kabutack, Rainbow Sea, Spirit, and all those brilliant works from the turn of the millennium. I once drew my own little booklet, filling it with characters I had invented — each with elaborate armors, factions, power levels, elemental attributes. I loved it. I loved imagining.
But then, as schoolwork mounted and my parents' expectations grew, I began to transform into a more worldly kind of student. You understand my drift — I started performing for the gaze of the world instead of sinking daily into my own imagined universe.
And so it went, all the way through high school. During that time, I discovered Haruki Murakami, Yasunari Kawabata, Ryūnosuke Akutagawa, and Natsume Sōseki. I read The Catcher in the Rye and The Great Gatsby — my favorite novel — along with One Hundred Years of Solitude and The Alchemist. Some of Keigo Higashino's novels too, though Murakami I read the most. There is something devastatingly beautiful about the sorrow and solitude in Japanese literature. At the same time, The Catcher in the Rye, Farewell Waltz, and Mark Twain gave me a profound sense of comfort and exhilaration. I loved the rush of words flowing through my mind, the imagination they ignited.
Then came university. The Hengshui-style cramming had drained me so completely that I arrived with neither the will nor the ambition to study. I had fought my way from a poverty-stricken county to Zhejiang University — everything was a first. My classmates had monthly allowances of forty thousand yuan. And I realized: no matter the university, no matter the country, there are only two kinds of people — the poor and the rich. Politics exists to control the masses; the poor can only birth more poor. All of society's resources, including countless beautiful women, exist to serve those at the top. Your genes and the family you are born into — that is fate.
I thought university would be luminous with future promise. Instead, the real university demanded GPA, grad school applications, PhD tracks, internship grinding. When does it end? I began skipping classes. Step by step, I walked myself into academic probation. I am in it now — still owing core courses like Calculus and Data Structures. I have been insomniac for two years. I survived the most agonizing period of my life. Now I am preparing to apply to schools abroad. I have neither a good sleep routine nor a fitness habit. I am overweight, and I am ugly. My major is Information Management and Information Systems.
Music saved my life.
I found Kanye, Travis, Drake, Lil Wayne, Don. I found David Tao, Khalil Fong, Cheer Chen, Omnipotent Youth Society, Pink Floyd. I found YMO. I found ZARD. I found Henan Rap God. These artists' music, along with my lifelong love of photography, literature, Ye's fashion sense — they have been elevating my aesthetic. Only when I stumble upon videos about taste on Douyin do I feel genuinely interested.
This is me, as I stand now:
Lost. Unrepentant. Inferior and arrogant at once. A contradiction.
